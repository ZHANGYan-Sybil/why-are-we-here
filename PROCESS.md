# Process

## AI Tools and How I Used Them

I used two AI assistants during the development of this essay: **Doubao** and **ChatGPT**.

I first used Doubao in Chinese to organise my initial thoughts. I described my ideas, experiences, and questions in my own words, and asked it to help me turn these into clearer and more coherent Chinese writing. I then brought this material to ChatGPT, mainly for English translation and language refinement.

After that, I gave ChatGPT the full assignment brief and asked it to check whether my essay met the requirements. This process helped me realise that my initial draft was too general and did not contain enough personal reflection. I then reconsidered the structure of the essay and decided to separate the general argument from a more personal section about what programming and AI meant for me.

For the personal section, I described my own experiences with a guitar-tab visualisation project and learning the Blueprint system in Unreal Engine 5. I explained these experiences to ChatGPT in Chinese, and used it to organise and translate them into English while keeping the ideas and experiences my own.

Finally, I gave the complete essay to ChatGPT to check for structural, language, formatting, and citation-related problems. 

## Something I Kept

One passage I kept from ChatGPT's revision came from my experience learning the Blueprint system in Unreal Engine 5. My original description was quite conversational and imprecise:

> “When I was trying to learn the Blueprint system in Unreal Engine 5, I also tried to get help from AI. It could probably guide me when I was working on a small function, but it could not understand my whole project structure or how it worked with other levels and functions. The lack of context made me feel quite helpless and frustrated during development. It also made me realise that I cannot just wait for AI to become capable of handling Blueprint systems. I need to develop this ability myself.”

I kept the revised version because it expressed the same experience much more precisely:

> “I experienced something similar when I started learning the Blueprint system in Unreal Engine 5. AI could be helpful when I wanted to implement a small and isolated function, but the situation became much more difficult when the problem involved the wider structure of my project. A Blueprint does not exist on its own; it interacts with other Blueprints, levels, functions, variables, and systems. AI could suggest solutions to individual problems, but it did not always have enough context to understand how those solutions would affect the rest of my project. This often left me feeling frustrated and powerless, because I was waiting for AI to understand a system that I did not yet understand well enough myself.”

The original description came directly from my own experience, but it was not yet a clear argument. ChatGPT helped turn my frustration into a more precise explanation of what I had learned from it. I especially kept the final sentence, because it captures my main realisation in a simple and direct way: I am not learning programming because AI cannot do these things, but because I want to be able to work with AI without losing control of the process.

## Something I Rejected

One thing I rejected was an earlier conclusion generated by ChatGPT:

> “In the age of AI-assisted coding, learning programming does not mean that designers need to write every line of code themselves or compete with AI in terms of speed. Instead, programming gives creators the knowledge needed to understand, control, and question computational tools.
>
> For contemporary designers and artists, programming literacy is therefore not about competing with AI. It is about maintaining creative agency. By understanding how code works, creators can use AI as a powerful assistant while still making the decisions that shape the meaning, direction, and outcome of their work.”

I thought this conclusion was correct and consistent with the overall argument of my essay. However, I rejected it because it was too broad. It explained what programming literacy means for designers in general, but it did not say enough about what I had personally experienced or why I had reached this conclusion.

I therefore asked ChatGPT to revise the conclusion based on my own experiences, particularly my difficulties with the guitar visualisation project and Unreal Engine 5 Blueprint. I explained that my frustration came from realising that I could not simply wait for AI to become capable enough to solve these problems for me. I needed to develop enough understanding of the systems myself to work with AI effectively.

The revised conclusion became:

> “I do not need to learn programming because AI cannot do these things. I need to learn it because I want to be able to work with AI without losing control of the process. The more capable AI becomes, the more important it may be for me to understand the systems I am asking it to operate within. I do not need to become a professional programmer, but I do need enough programming literacy to install and organise tools, understand the structure of a system, communicate my intentions precisely, diagnose problems, and decide whether an AI-generated solution actually moves my work in the direction I want.”

I kept this version because it expresses a conclusion that comes from my own experience rather than making a general statement about designers and AI. The original version was reasonable, but the revised version is more specific to why I am learning programming and what I want that knowledge to give me.
